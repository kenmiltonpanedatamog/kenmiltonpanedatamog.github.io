Suffering and Morality

Published on September 11, 2026

2026   ·   humss   ·   essays

(This is an uncompleted essay written on 2026-08-19)

Suffering, the thought that there are people and animals experiencing pain and hardship, have been on my mind for weeks I suppose. It has been raining in that period of thinking, and I really had a lot of thoughts about the morality aspect of those suffering- specifically our moral obligation as individuals to help as much as we can, or at least start thinking about the suffering of others to hopefully inspire ourselves to make a contribution.

Earlier today, I watched Peter Singer’s talk at the Talks at Google YouTube Channel. I had nothing to do and my thoughts about suffering and morality just made me interested enough to watch it. Just a few weeks ago, around July, I read his famous essay talking about our moral obligation as individuals to alleviate suffering.

There are endless types of suffering, different people define and feel it differently. I’d like to define it myself, but I want to focus on human suffering for this brief essay. I do believe that certain animals do suffer, but I’d like to talk about it in a different essay. I define human suffering as:

  1. The lack of basic survival and safety needs which are food and water, shelter, medicine and healthcare.
  2. The lack of liberty and safety. Liberty is the situation where a person has the rights and protection not to be violated, under their country’s constitution and the accepted universal moral truths.
  3. The lack of emotional faculties such as a loving family and friends, and a good self identity.

In Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs, my definition would cover the Physiological, Safety, and Love & Belonging levels. My definition of suffering is limited to the physical and emotional aspects of a person. I believe that a person who still lacks the two definitions of suffering I made are in a desperate need to be helped.

Just imagine the first one, despite the conveniences of the modern world, there are still people who only eat once a day or not at all, there are still people who live on the streets and beg, and there are still people who cannot access the medications and health services to ease their conditions and diseases. The suffering they are experiencing physically is unimaginable for most people. We feel everyday that our own “suffering” is the worst, yet there are people alive who are just alive because they are still somehow breathing. The concept of happiness is not even conceivable in my opinion when one does not have consistent access to the first set of needs. I don’t believe any optimist who would say that people can be happy when they cannot even survive like most pets. I acknowledge that there are people who are just lacking one or two of these needs at some times, and we might naturally consider them to suffer less, but the point still stands that these basic needs must be met all the time.

Next, the requirement of liberty and safety is another fundamental requirement to not suffer. Despite the world abandoning its barbaric practices in favor of more peaceful ones in general, there are still violations to peace happening all over the world. People suffer when they are use as slaves, detained, raped, abused, and more bad things done to them. These are what I consider liberty and safety violations, which require not a thing to be given, but for a thing not to be done. There are people out there that are living a good life and yet violated, and are therefore, have suffered because of bad things done to them.

Lastly, there is the invisible suffering which resides in people’s brains. Our concept of suffering can also be felt even if we are in a safe place and with abundant basic needs. Mental health, or better called emotional health can be the greatest source of suffering to some. Psychologists cannot cure anyone of mental suffering, as they say. But why is that? It’s because we are complex, thinking beings. And it comes with it the toll on having the consciousness to suffer inside our heads. This type of suffering is the less understood one to this day, and some still think it is non existent. The emotional faculties I’m talking about are the people and ideas of ourselves which facilitates a better state of mind. I don’t want to talk about psychology since I don’t have a background on that, but I believe that one of the most important faculties is the presence of a loving family, which includes friends. The love which they bring to a person is so important to their self identity. ‘I am loved’, is perhaps the best way you can describe yourself.

I’d like to start this essay by talking about our role as individuals to be aware and take action amidst the many suffering in the world. Feeling sorry for people who suffer is the first step before we can take action. Just knowing and feeling empathetic is the first step to making a helpful contribution, even if it takes years before you take the first action.

For many people including me, the taking action part feels impossible or at least hard for the time being. Students like me, who have no jobs nor rich parents, cannot just go around helping people. We could volunteer, but we also have school and our own problems as growing adults which needs our time. I’m not reasoning myself to be moral despite not doing anything to help yet. I’m saying this because it is an important moral claim- that it is okay not to do anything yet, it is enough to simply be aware and be willing to help when opportunities come in the future.

One of the problems that arises when people read Singer’s essay is the implication that we ought to sacrifice every luxury to save people. Personally, I do believe in the essence of it, but what defines luxury? Is it morally bad to prioritize buying a big and beautiful house for your family instead of donating the money to charity? Singer’s concept of “equal moral worth” makes it hard to draw the line between what is enough for us and what ought to be given. But certainly, there are many luxuries people have such as having many mansions without people to live it all, and having net worth in the millions and billions which are extreme cases of luxuries which can easily be said to be less of a moral worth. I cannot draw the line for people, but personally, I have a line which I think makes sense to me, and people should have their own too.

Singer’s call to action- that we have a moral obligation to sacrifice luxuries to help people still holds. But there is a time when people cannot help, and it’s okay. It is a ‘blameless wrongdoing’ for people not to help, just because they still haven’t met the line of luxury, whatever that means to them.

So what should we do?
