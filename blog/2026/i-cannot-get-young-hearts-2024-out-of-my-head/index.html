I cannot get Young Hearts (2024) out of my head

Published on September 13, 2026

2026   ·   media   musings   2025-12-27   ·   archives

I watched Young Hearts yesterday because I finally searched it up after seeing the opening up/car scene on Instagram. Now that I’ve watched it, I cannot seem to get it out of my head. I don’t know if it is because of the beautiful and wonderful actors that played the main roles, or because I can’t move on from the movie’s idealistic story. Just for the record, I watched Close (2022) today, and damn, it was the complete opposite. But we are talking about Young Hearts here.

When the movie started, I was immediately drawn to Elias, he is a fine man but is still very much a kid. What will unravel as the movie progresses is the early age in which the two lead characters fell in love and also accepted themselves being in love with the same gender. They are only 14 years old in the film, and when I try to remember what I felt when I was 14, I just remember myself liking boys but is still pretty much hiding my identity, which is still the case now. When Alexander told Elias about his first experience of falling in love, I was shocked when he said it was a boy. Elias did not say anything to hopefully know his ideas of such things at an early age, but Alexander seems to have accepted already. Alexander never mentioned the word “gay or bi” to describe himself until one scene later in the film where Elias contrasted himself as not being Alexander which he called “gay”. Even the friends of both Elias and Alexander seem to have this idea already about homosexual love. It seems like Elias is the only one who does not know or accept it yet.

I think the turning point of their relationship was the barn scene, where they were caught up by rain and went to the barn for a roof. That scene itself screams the intimacy of young love. Elias has been looking at Alexander throughout the film, looking at him in a very close and intimate way already, but this scene cemented that looking into love. Alexander just kissed Elias as they looked very close at each other. This is another proof for me that Alexander is way ahead of his age for things like this.

But here comes man vs society but more like man vs self. Elias, still not saying anything about his love for Alexander, felt the need to now show it when people are around. When Alexander hugged Elias in front of their schoolmates and Elias turned him away, it was a turning point for them but more for Elias because we can see again how Alexander has accepted himself, while Elias still needs to accept himself too. When Alexander and Elias talked about it, we can see Alexander asking if Elias really likes him or not and Elias finally saying yes. But this yes is still coupled with some sort of anxiety about how society will react about their relationship. Again, Alexander does not care about this while Elias still is. This scene is the climax for me, as it made Elias journey into self-acceptance.

Elias, while obviously having a feeling for Alexander, is still with his girlfriend. She later discovered Elias and Alexander’s affection when she saw them sort of hugging. She just knew upon seeing them that close. You gotta feel bad at least for the girl, but you cannot also blame Elias for not revealing his feelings for Alexander as he himself seems not to know the answer yet. Previously, there was a scene of Elias and his girlfriend being together and sleeping together, and Elias waking up, and rushing to Alexander’s house. Maybe this is the point where Elias knew that he was more in love with Alexander. But this scene is one that is hard to watch too. Elias attempted to kiss Alexander, but Alexander did not let him. I don’t know what message this scene is supposed to make. I’m conflicted whether this is Alexander keeping his distance as he felt distanced when Elias pushed him away, or it is some sort of sign that when Elias finally knows he loves him that he also lost that love for him.

The succeeding minutes will make you feel sad, and also worried. I was worried that I would be heartbroken as well by the end of the movie. I thought that it would all go down from this point on. But I’m wrong. This movie has an environment that is so ideal, that the only problem was Elias not accepting himself.

Their fight did not end with that. When Elias and Alexander are both at a party with their friends. We will witness them drifting from each other. At first, Elias just kissed his girlfriend (which is still a bit questionable since it is unclear whether they broke up or not). Alexander knew this, but they just went on to the party until it went south. Elias broke out of anger or denial when Alexander did a dare to kiss another boy. To be honest, what happened next really tainted Elias a bit. When they kissed, emotions flooded Elias and stormed out. Their friends followed him, along with Alexander. Elias broke out, saying things like “I am not like him [Alexander], I am not gay.” The exact words are better known when you watch this movie. But what Elias said definitely tainted their relationship as Alexander heard all of it. Alexander pushed Elias and cursed him. (I left a very important point where Elias and Alexander journeyed together because it is also better known when you watch the movie, but I also felt that it just showed a glimpse of what Alexander’s environment is that made him accept himself.)

I really thought it would have a bad ending.

After that fight, Elias went to isolation. He did not go home nor in school but instead went to his grandfather. They went on a trip together, wherein we saw Elias finally confess about his love for Alexander, although he said it without much words. His grandfather accepted him wholeheartedly, which adds to the idealism of this movie. That kind of acceptance from others is very much not the case in many countries and families, yet Elias just got accepted for the first time without any negative reactions. Then, we arrive at the car scene, where Elias opened up to his mother. Like the grandfather, this scene showed his mother totally accepting him. In this scene, we finally hear from Elias himself, that he is in love with Alexander. Previously we just assumed based on their closeness, but we finally hear a word: Elias loves Alexander.

Now, the ending. Elias now accepts himself, his family accepting him as well (the dad did later but still the same wholehearted and idealistic acceptance), he is finally ready to show that he is indeed in love with Alexander. We can see him in the end, hanging out with his friends, including his still unclear whether they broke up girlfriend. But when Elias saw Alexander, he sprinted out and got hit. His girlfriend went to him, Elias saying he saw Alexander, and we see his (I will now end them myself) ex-girlfriend freeing him in a way. His brother did the same next, so Elias was ready to see Alexander since they fought. Finally, they saw each other among the crowd.

Elias went to Alexander, Alexander smiled, then Elias kissed him. Alexander’s father is shown to be so happy (which once again shows this wholehearted acceptance), we can also see Elias’ mother smiling, and finally Elias’ father sees his son kissing another man. I thought his father would be the one to break the mood, but he completed it. The whole family accepted them both. And we saw them finally being happy together at that moment. The movie ended with a joyful play between the friend group of Elias and Alexander, with the two of them riding a bike. Alexander is pedaling while Elias is in the back, hugging him. What a beautiful and idealistic movie indeed. It is a movie that saw a character accept what he felt, without too much backlash.
