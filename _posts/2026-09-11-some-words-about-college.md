My first semester of college will officially end tomorrow, as I take my very last exams. Time flew by so fast that August 11, 2025, felt like half a year ago. I should have written here and there about my college experiences, but I never got around to writing consistently. I am sure I have some written fragments here and there, but I never sat down and wrote a complete essay, which I will do now.

Where should I start?

Maybe I'll start with the first day of classes, which was August 11. I wore a green polo there. And as I entered the campus, I met A and we saw R. I said hi, and R's friend, G, will become my very first friend. As I am waiting to enter my first class which is GMATH, I learned that G is also a CS student, and so we talked. I don't even know how I socialized that well to get a friend already to be honest, and looking in hindsight, I really hit the jackpot. For our first class, we did an introduce yourself. We said our name, our past school, why we chose Computer Science and why chose Saint Louis University, and answered: What is the importance of GMATH? When it was my turn, I was nervous but I managed to introduce myself without shaming myself. I answered why SLU with "gusto ko magpakalayo," my classmates laughed, but before I said that, my teacher also asked why did I not go to DMMMSU since there is a CS there. I later learned that he is a graduate of DMMMSU as well. In our second class, we met G. I remember us talking about computers and I was able to say that I use Linux, we did talk about Linux. The teacher never came to meet us but I have two friends now. Then, the same happened to our third class; we waited and talked. As we were waiting, we saw a boy standing alone on the bridge near the amphitheater. I think it was G who initiated a talk, so we are four now. We waited and talked until the class decided to leave. As we headed to leave the school, my newfound friends initiated a fist bump as a sign of goodbye, and that became a habit to us whenever we part our ways. We created a group chat that night, still named "K, G, G" on my side. I had three friends on the first day of school, which is a big accomplishment for me. Today, December 12, as I am writing this portion, we are still friends.

On the second day of classes, it was scheduled as our Orientation at the Main Campus, we had to go there before 7 AM. So, with my new friends, we went there. Only me, K, and G were together in the morning since G was late. But in the afternoon, I was with A. The whole experience was surreal to me. I did feel like I do not belong at times as I was surrounded by unfamiliar faces, but I did not allow that to undermine the positives. It is a privilege to study here in SLU, not because I think it is the best school, but because I am studying away from home, and for me that is the dream.

Before I even passed the SLU CEE, I already had a plan in my head that I would study either in UP, or UC for its trimester. I wanted to study in Baguio because I wanted to grow alone, to experience life without the constant watch of my parents. Since I was in elementary, I was used to having my Mama watch me since she is a teacher in my elementary school and I even had her as a teacher in 2nd and 4th grade. I was eventually given more freedom in high school since I now study at a different municipality, but at the end of the day, I was still going home. My desire to be alone and have more freedom is a dream to me, not as a sign of selfishness but as a desire to truly grow myself. I thought that going at home in college means I am still bound to the same habits and same environment. I thought that to truly become successful, I have to taste true freedom, which I have now. This plan of mine did not get cemented until I convinced my Mama. When I told her that I want to study in Baguio, she advised me to just study in DMMMSU, where I graduated senior high school. I remember saying to her that "I will not meet people... I will not grow when I have to go home everyday...". I was anxious for about a month, as my future is still not according to my desires. Yet somehow, my Mama with the advice of my Titas as she said to me, decided that I will study in SLU, not in UC since my dream of studying in UP is not granted as I failed the UPCAT. I don't actually have any plans before to study in SLU as I really wanted that UC trimester but my Mama did not want me to study in UC for some reason and in hindsight, I'm glad I went to SLU. I don't know what my Tita A or Tita A told my Mama, but I'm glad she changed her mind.

I went from being anxious that I will have to settle for my old school to being excited for my future in SLU. Just to clarify, my reason for not wanting to go to DMMMSU is just the close distance to our home and the mere fact that I would have to go home everyday. I don't think that SLU or any school in Baguio is superior. I just wanted some distance, and more freedom. I wanted to live alone. I know that it is a privilege. So I am committed to not waste this opportunity.

On the first week of classes from August 13 onward, I did feel a lot of things. First, I felt alien to this new environment. I had to live alone, which presents various challenges. I do not have the convenience of having my parents do things for me. So I had to do a lot of things myself. When I got to school, I would sometimes feel like I should not be here. But I was quick to adapt to a new routine, like walking for 15 minutes to my school and back to my dorm. I still feel jealous at times, but I am more than grateful for what I have now.

I also felt dumb, or to describe it better, I finally felt like I am not one of the smartest in the room. I was really humbled since from my elementary days until my senior high school, I felt like I was smart because I got good grades and even topped my class in my last year in senior high school. I know that I am bad at math, but my good grades kept me thinking that I am smart enough to be considered one. Upon meeting my classmates, I already saw some people who are way beyond me in terms of "smartness" and also practical abilities. They speak so fluently, they are so good at math, and they are also way advanced than me in programming and technological know-how. I finally met people I've been wanting to meet. Noticeably, I met someone who uses Linux as well and damn, he is way ahead of me. My friends alone are smarter than me in some ways. And I like it this way. I am finally a small fish surrounded by bigger fishes, which I know will motivate me to improve and grow bigger myself. Did this last paragraph make me sound like I'm big-headed? I hope not to sound that way. I am not bragging about myself being "smart", I'm just saying that I considered myself smart since I've always had good grades. I am also not saying that I never met smarter people before, it's just that I finally met the smart people in the niche things I am interested about, not just the smart who got good grades. But regardless, college indeed humbles you.

And most felt of all, I felt in control. I experienced being an adult at last. To me, this is the most beautiful part of going to college.

Aside from meeting friends and my classmates, I also met some of the best teachers I've ever met yet. Our major subject professors challenged us in ways that are not easy nor impossible. They were able to give us the best lectures, and I felt that every class was completely worth it. Some teachers also left more than knowledge to me personally, some of them became little inspirations, and some left impacting messages and lessons that I'll definitely remember.

To Sir V:
Thank you for the world-class lectures every single time you enter our class. I've always felt this immense sense of understanding every time you utter a word. Your occasional jokes are funny and relevant. Although your subject was the first time I got a line of 7, I still think that I learned so much in Discrete Mathematics and it was my fault for not taking math seriously in my formative years. That 99 prelim grade really made me think that I can take this easy, but what a turnaround. Your Christmas message to us really left something in me.

To Ma'am J:
Thank you for the challenge. You made Introduction to Programming challenging, but as you said "it is not impossible". I've enjoyed every lab activity, and I'll remember my low scores in quizzes that pushed me to do better next time. When I saw how knowledgeable our programming professor is, that is you, I felt validated for choosing to study here.

To Ma'am B:
Thank you for the motivation, thank you for always reminding us how we should take this first year as a practice for our higher years. Thank you for always relating our seemingly boring Introduction to Computing lessons to our future endeavors. I'll remember the disciplinary lessons you have given us at times.
