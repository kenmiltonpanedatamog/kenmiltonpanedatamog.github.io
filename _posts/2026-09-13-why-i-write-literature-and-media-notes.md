Staring at a blank page makes me want to be the best writer that ever lived. Although the great writers often died long before their written thoughts became known, I’d like to think that most of them never intended their writing to be seen in the first place. Now, writing literature (written) notes feels like a meta-practice, it is essentially writing about someone else’s writings. Similarly, writing media (non-written) notes is just writing about the creative creations of others, be it in the form of a movie, a show, or others. But why separate "literature" and "media"? The reason for that is simple: the media is the message. A book will convey a different message than a limited-by-runtime movie, but anyways, it doesn’t really matter.

The following reasons for writing these notes are listed below in no particular order:

## Second Brain

Having a so called “second brain” is just a fancy way of saying this: My brain will forget, but I don’t want to forget, thus I need a way for my thoughts to be preserved indefinitely.

Any system of preserving our thoughts other than remembering them will serve the purpose of mitigating our lack of an indefinite memory. In the past, this was done by carving messages into stones or printing on paper. But in our digital age, it has never been easier to store an abundant amount of information conveniently. Digital data don’t forget or just disappear by design, but technology still fail, so the information we store on digital mediums is still indefinite. But it is better than our own memory.

## Meditating on different ideas

Ideas come and go, and with the advent of technology, it has never been easier to flood our minds with information, it can be useless information or a useful one, but nonetheless both will just come and go. If we don’t take the time to think and meditate about them, then we are just consuming.

What I’m trying to do by writing these notes is to have a reason to meditate upon useful ideas that I come across. It should hold me accountable by forcing my lazy brain to slow down and digest what I’m learning from my daily consumption of information. Consuming content is the same as amusing ourselves to death (Postman reference). I’ve consumed enough in my lifetime, but I meditated very little. I don’t want to die one day and regret not taking the time to think, and be creative.

## Being creative

There is creativity within us, we just have to find a medium and a reason to unleash it. Not everyone will consider themselves as a creative person, but how sad will life be without a little creative space? I personally feel a deep creative passion inside me, and I believe everyone has it deep inside them as well.

To unleash this deep creative desire of mine, I’ve tried many ways of creating and sharing it. I have written pages upon pages of journals, wrote essays when I feel like “being creative,” and also tried some ways of sharing it, but it never got consistent. Writing and sharing my notes or essays on various things I consume is one way to be creative consistently. I will never stop consuming, so I want to never stop being creative as well.

## Sharing my thoughts

Lastly, what is the purpose of being creative when I’m the only one who can enjoy my work? While the motivation and purpose for taking notes is largely for my personal fulfillment, it does not hurt to share my thoughts. Publishing my notes allows me to share what I learn, and think to others. I don’t claim my insights to be largely important, I don’t share because I think I am influential in any sense. But to those who will stumble upon my writings, and will find them useful for their own lives, it is my pleasure to have you read my ramblings.

So welcome to this blog, where I share my notes on various literature (books, articles, and others…) and media (movies, shows, and others).
