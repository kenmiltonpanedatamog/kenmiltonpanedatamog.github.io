I vaguely remember a poem we used to recite every some afternoon when I was in 6th grade, I remember a fragment of it saying—Am I the same girl? I used to delight... the cookies and ice cream, the pastries mother baked. That poem, by an unknown author, still has me questioning myself from time to time—Am I the same boy? Or have I stayed the same, still the kid I once was.

People change, all the time—so was I. I hold a strong opinion that the majority of my childhood changed compared to now. But I still like the idea that some things never changed.

I've grown to be the same kid, who just get good grades. From Elementary until Senior High School, and I hope until College. Good grades to me have always meant having an Honor, having a 90+ GWA became my standard of good enough, that getting a line of 8 means a failure to me. I don't like to call myself smart, but if having Honors means that, then I am smart since 1st grade. But since my idea of being smart changed some time in High School, I saw grades as just a number you get for getting good scores. Good scores that, most of the time, just requires you to memorize facts. But I am not a genius, my grades in math haven't always been up to my standards and my voice still shakes when I am talking to people—I hope this changes.

I've grown to believe what I now believe. Let's talk about religion, when you are born in a predominantly Catholic country, you are bound to become one. As a great writer wrote before I was even born—"There is no such thing as a Christian child: only a child of Christian parents." So when I was born, I was baptized, and the rest is history. If you already know what I'm going to confess, great. But I am... not religious—I am an agnostic atheist since 2021. This belief of mine towards religion is just a personal realization of the conflicting ideas religion has and my own views of the world. I still celebrate Christmas out of respect, I still go to church when my family and friends invite me to, I still pray to someone when I am vulnerable. Yet, I am an atheist—I hope people understand that I mean no harm towards their own religious views, and I believe we should be free to believe whatever brings us peace.

I've grown to be more comfortable feeling what I feel. Here is another confession, I am not straight. I've always had a feeling back then that I may be gay or "bakla," as they call us, yet because of societal expectations, I just shrugged it off and forced myself to conform to what is allowed—that a man should only fall in love with a woman, and vice versa. This idea, that I do like both, is still queer to me. To be honest, I am not fond of calling myself a specific spectrum of the LGBT+ because what I know I feel is simple. I am attracted to men physically, but I am still open to having the expected relationship with my opposite. I am a male, call me he and him, that will never change—And I don't want to complicate things further because I am comfortable expressing how I feel this way, but again, respect should be given to those who might feel otherwise.

I've also grown to see myself change stances in social, ethical, and philosophical matters. As I grew older, and got more aware, it should be given that my views change and grow as well. The facts we think are true may well change in the future. Also, I find people to be a joke sometimes. I find it funny how people can claim like they know it, and claim that a thing is good or bad, so they leave no room for others to question their opinions. I think people lack the openness to be wrong—and this is the easiest way to be a fool.

Change will always happen. In nature, in society, in ourselves. So to answer the question: Am I the same boy?

The answer is no, but I am still here. So I am free to believe that some things never change.
